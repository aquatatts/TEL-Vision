# Getting the Ritual Duo out the door — the commercial architecture

Written Friday 11 September 2026, the morning after Chapter One opened. Based on the first
411 sessions, not on theory.

## What the data actually says

Sessions by landing page, Thursday + Friday:

| Landing page | Sessions |
|---|---|
| `/password` | **207** |
| `/` | 201 |
| `/products/tattoo-aftercare-kit` | **2** |
| `/policies/terms-of-service` | 1 |

Three findings, in order of how much they matter.

### 1. Half of all traffic hit a locked door

**207 sessions landed on `/password`** — people who came during Thursday while the gate was
still up, off the countdown sticker and the link in bio. They found a locked door and left.

Nothing to fix now, the gate is down. But it changes the denominator: **real post-launch
traffic was ~204 sessions, not 411.** One order against 204 is **0.5%**, not 0.24%. Still
low, but not the disaster the raw number implies.

It also proves the demand was there *before* the doors opened, which is the strongest signal
in the whole dataset.

### 2. Almost nobody reaches the product page

**201 landed on the homepage. Two landed on the product page.**

The link in bio points at the homepage. So warm traffic — people who already follow Benny,
already trust the work, already know what this is — arrives at a long brand story and has to
navigate to the thing they can actually buy. Every extra click costs people.

The homepage is doing its job beautifully. It is just not the right door for this audience.

### 3. Zero abandoned checkouts

Out of 411 sessions, the only abandoned cart all week was Ben's own test.

That rules out the entire bottom of the funnel. Nobody is bouncing off shipping cost, the
payment step, or a broken checkout — because almost nobody is getting there. **The drop is at
"do I want this", not "will I pay for this."**

## The thing that explains all three

**Aftercare is a trigger purchase, not an impulse purchase.**

Nobody wakes up wanting tattoo balm. They want it in a specific window: they have just booked
a session, they are sitting in the chair, or they walked out an hour ago with cling film on.
Outside that window the best aftercare in the world is a nice-looking jar.

That is why the counter at Squires Ink sells it — **the trigger is built into the room.**
Orders #1042 and #1043 were both full price, both across the counter, no marketing attached.
And it is why Instagram does not sell it: scrolling is not a trigger. Those 200 people came
to look at what Benny built. Admiration is not intent.

**So the job is not to make the website convert harder. It is to be present at the trigger.**

## The five systems, in build order

### System 1 · Shorten the path — today, free

Point the **link in bio at `/products/tattoo-aftercare-kit`**, not the homepage. This
audience is already warm on the brand; they do not need the story first, they need the
product. The homepage stays exactly as it is for anyone who wants the whole thing.

One change, thirty seconds, and it is the highest ratio of impact to effort available.

**Do not put the discount link there.** `TELTAKEOVER` is uncapped, so a public discount URL
hands $49.99 to everyone and makes the founding-price segmentation pointless.

### System 2 · Capture, so a trigger can be reached later

Those ~204 visitors are not buyers today. Some of them book a piece next month, and that is
the moment they convert — but only if there is a way to reach them.

Right now there is not: **411 sessions produced one contactable person.** The build sheet is
in `CAPTURE.md`. The mid-page band is done and committed and needs publishing; the popup and
back-in-stock are next.

Capture is not a marketing nicety here. It is the only mechanism that turns a trigger you
missed into a sale you get.

### System 3 · The artist channel — the one that scales

This is the big one, and it is the natural extension of what already works.

The counter sells because an artist hands it over at the trigger moment. **Every other
artist's chair is the same moment.** Aftercare brands do not scale through consumers, they
scale through the people holding the machine — an artist recommending a product at the end of
a session converts at a rate no ad will ever touch.

Start where trust already exists: Valerio, then the rest of Squires, then studios Benny knows
on the Coast. What it needs before that conversation:
- A pro/wholesale price and minimum
- A reason for the artist to care — margin, or their clients healing better and rebooking
- The compliance work that a stockist will ask for (see below)

### System 4 · Search — currently at one session

**One organic search session out of 411.** For a product whose customers literally type
"how do I look after a new tattoo" into Google, that is a standing miss.

The Healing Guide is already written and it is genuinely good — day-by-day, honest about
petrolatum, no fluff. It is sitting on a page nobody can find. That content deserves to be
found by people at the exact moment of maximum intent, and search is the only channel where
the customer arrives already triggered.

Slowest of the five to pay off, and the only one that compounds.

### System 5 · Retargeting — once the pool is real

The Meta pixel is connected and the catalog channel is live. The audience is ~400 people,
which is thin — a website Custom Audience needs closer to 1,000 to perform, and a Lookalike
source wants 1,000–5,000.

**Do not run ads yet.** With capture broken, paid traffic pours into the same bucket and
leaves the same way. Fix Systems 1 and 2, get two or three more content moments like Thursday
into the pixel, then retarget people who already came. That is the cheapest money in the
business — and it is worthless a month early.

## What has to be true before the artist channel

The compliance items are no longer "after launch" — they are the gate on System 3, because a
stockist will ask:

- Products liability insurance (broker call)
- AICIS registration check
- PET and stability testing before the 1,000-set run

## The honest summary

Thursday did not fail. It proved reach — 373 social sessions from a standing start on a brand
that did not publicly exist that morning — and it proved the store works end to end with real
money.

What it exposed is that **the website was built to convert traffic that arrives already
wanting the product, and Instagram does not send that traffic.** The counter does. Other
artists' counters would. Search would.

The website is not the problem. It is the destination. The systems above are the roads to it.
