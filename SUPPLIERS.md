### The 8 September document drop — 11 attachments, still unread in Gmail

Equity & manufacturing relationship certificate · business licences for both companies (×3

fragrance allergen declaration. Added to the six already held (ISO 22716, FDA GMP, 2× MSDS,

**This is a real due-diligence pack** — the kind a broker, a stockist's compliance officer, or
AICIS record-keeping expects. It is not far off complete.

### What to verify when the files are opened

| File | The check that decides it |
|---|---|
| Equity & manufacturing relationship | Names **both** entities and states the relationship — and is it self-declared by VOG or issued by an authority? Self-declared is much weaker. Chop, signature, date. |
| Business licences ×3 | 18-character Unified Social Credit Code present; **Heyan's business scope includes cosmetics manufacturing**; addresses match Huadu / Baiyun; both current. |
| COA, both products | **Do the batch numbers match the codes physically printed on the jars now being sold?** That was the ask. "Updated" is not the same as "matching". |
| MSDS, both products | The original defect was both showing **Bulk Code CC-1**. If the reissued pair still share a code, the same problem has been re-sent. |
| INCI, both products | Must match the jars **and** the website. Jar-vs-website was already verified identical, so any mismatch here means VOG's own document is the odd one out. |
| Fragrance allergen declaration | Needs **named allergens with percentages** (linalool, limonene, geraniol…). AU/EU labelling threshold is 0.001% leave-on. "Contains allergens" with no numbers is not usable. |

### Four gaps that are confirmed, not suspected

1. **ISO 22716 scope for the balm — unanswered.** The question was whether the ISO certificate
   covers an anhydrous balm, since its scope wording reads "creams, lotions, liquids and gels".
   VOG answered about the **Chinese production licence** instead. Different document, and the
   ISO one is what a stockist or insurer actually reads.

2. **No stability study.** Confirmed in writing. The COA's −10°C/+40°C range is a *process*
   measurement taken during production, not stability data.

3. **No preservative efficacy test (PET).** Confirmed. *"The preservative system we use can
   maintain a 3-year shelf life. However, we have not conducted a preservative efficacy test."*
   That makes the three-year figure an assertion.

   ⚠️ **The website states it publicly** — the FAQs page reads *"Every batch is tested … with a
   three-year shelf life."* That is an unsubstantiated claim on a live storefront under
   Australian Consumer Law. Either substantiate it or change the sentence.

   **Good news:** PET and stability both run on **finished product**. VOG's formula is not
   required, so their vague "you can conduct the test yourself" is not a blocker. Send jars to
   an Australian lab.

4. **No manufacturer product liability insurance.** *"We don't have product liability insurance
   covering export markets. We are merely a manufacturer engaged in production, not an
   exporter."*

   Two consequences, both material:
   - **Aqua747 carries 100% of the product liability risk.** No upstream insurer to claim
     against, on a product applied to open wounds.
   - **"Not an exporter" means TEL is the importer** — which settles the AICIS question.
     **TEL is the introducer and needs to register.**

## JAJI Parfumerie (Brisbane) — the fragrance

Jason West and James ("Jim") Alary. Boutique at 68 Racecourse Road, Hamilton. ABN 32886869536.
They make and retail their own line — Shadow Parfum, diffusers, candles — so they are perfumers
with their own product, not brokers. Client work claimed across perfume labels, hotels and
high-end property developments. USA arm in San Francisco.

Note "Australia's Master Perfumers" is a marketing title, not an accreditation — no Australian
body confers it. Not a knock; just not a credential.

### Their answers to the three questions

1. **Work to a brief, meet in person?** Yes. Consultation, iterative rounds, Brisbane meetings.
2. **Do we own the formula?** **No.** *"the underlying fragrance formula and its associated
   intellectual property remain the property of the perfumer… we do not transfer the underlying
   formula IP or disclose the technical raw material composition, supplier identities or
   individual percentages."* TEL receives SDS, IFRA certificate, allergen documentation, the
   olfactive structure for marketing, and bulk oil. Own manufacturer can be appointed.
3. **MOQ?** **5 litres of pure fragrance oil**, set by the third-party lab rather than by JAJI.
   No fixed lead time offered; regulatory assessment alone can take 6 weeks.

**Retained formula IP is standard industry practice** — the big houses all work this way. It is
not a red flag. But it makes JAJI a permanent single point of failure for TEL's signature scent:
every reorder, forever, comes through them.

**What 5 litres actually means:** at 20% concentration that is ~25 L of finished EdP, about
**500 × 50ml bottles**. At 15%, closer to 660. Almost exactly the shape of Chapter One.

**No pricing has been given at all across two emails.** That is the number that decides
everything and it is still missing.

### What to secure at the meeting, ranked

1. **Continuity / escrow.** If JAJI ceases trading, is acquired, or cannot supply, the formula
   releases to TEL or a nominated lab. The single most important ask.
2. **Every number.** Consultation fee, cost per round, rounds included, cost of extras,
   regulatory assessment, **price per litre of bulk oil**, and whether 5 L is also the *reorder*
   minimum.
3. **Exclusivity defined in the contract** — including close variants, not just the identical
   formula.
4. **Price protection** — a cap or CPI link, because TEL is a captive buyer after approval.
5. **The brief stays TEL's IP** — the olfactive direction should not be reusable for another
   tattoo or skincare brand.
6. **Two named references** to actually call.

## The one item that is not a tomorrow job

**Products liability insurance.** VOG carries none, TEL is the legal importer, and the product
goes onto open wounds. Until that policy exists, the entire exposure sits on Aqua747 Pty Ltd
with nothing behind it. It also gates the artist/stockist channel, since the first thing a
serious stockist asks for is the certificate.

Find a broker who handles **cosmetics specifically**. Brief them: aftercare product applied to
freshly tattooed skin, manufactured by a third party overseas to TEL's specification, imported
by TEL, sold direct and intended for wholesale.
